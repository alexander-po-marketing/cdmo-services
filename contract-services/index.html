Pharmaceutical contract manufacturing: how to shortlist the right partner

Choosing a contract manufacturing organization is rarely about one factor. The best CDMO services match technical capability, quality systems, and capacity with your development stage. Use the directory above to create a shortlist, then validate fit through focused questions.

1) Capability and scope

Confirm the provider’s actual scope (development, scale-up, validation, routine manufacturing, packaging, analytical support). “Yes, we do that” is not the same as “we do that routinely”.

2) Quality and regulatory readiness

For regulated supply, prioritize documented GMP experience and clear audit readiness. If your target markets include the EU or US, align expectations early (documentation style, data integrity practices, deviation management).

3) Scale and capacity

A provider can be technically perfect and still wrong if they can’t support your batch sizes or timeline. Filter by production scale to avoid mismatches.

4) Geography and supply chain

Region can matter for lead times, logistics, and risk management. If you require dual sourcing, shortlist across multiple regions and validate contingency options early.

Frequently asked questions about CDMO services

What is the difference between a CDMO and a CMO?

A CDMO typically supports development plus manufacturing. A CMO focuses more on manufacturing execution. In practice, many providers overlap, so verify the exact scope on their profile and in your first call.

CDMO vs CRO: are they the same?

No. CROs focus on research/services; CDMOs focus on development and manufacturing. Some companies offer both, especially around drug substance support.

How do I choose between Drug Substance and Drug Product categories?

Drug Substance is for APIs and upstream materials. Drug Product is for final dosage form manufacturing (tablets, injectables, etc.). Start with the one that matches your immediate sourcing need.

What information should I send to a CDMO company first?

Stage, target DS type or dosage form, batch sizes, timelines, target markets, and any must-have quality expectations (e.g., GMP). Clear inputs reduce back-and-forth and speed up feasibility checks.

Do I need GMP for early-stage development?

Not always. Requirements depend on stage and intended use. Many programs start non-GMP and transition later. Align expectations early so you don’t choose a partner that can’t scale with you.

Glossary

  • CDMO: Contract Development and Manufacturing Organization
  • CMO: Contract Manufacturing Organization
  • CRO: Contract Research Organization
  • Drug Substance (DS): the active ingredient (e.g., API, biologics DS)
  • Drug Product (DP): the final dosage form (tablets, injectables, etc.)
