What is natural extracts and products outsourcing (CDMO)?

Natural extract outsourcing focuses on producing drug substance from botanical or naturally derived sources. A pharmaceutical CDMO manages sourcing, extraction, purification, and standardization to deliver GMP-grade material.

Typical steps include raw material qualification, solvent or supercritical extraction, fractionation, and analytical standardization. Key constraints often involve raw material variability, contaminant control, and potency consistency.

When to choose a CDMO for natural extracts

  • You need reliable sourcing and standardization of botanical materials.
  • Extraction and purification require specialized equipment and analytics.
  • GMP documentation is required for regulated markets.

Common buyer requirements

  • Defined marker compounds, potency targets, and impurity limits.
  • Traceability and sustainability documentation for source materials.
  • Validated analytical methods for contaminants and residual solvents.

Natural extracts CDMO services FAQ

Practical answers for buyers sourcing CDMO services for natural extract drug substance manufacturing.

What does a natural extracts CDMO do for drug substance?

A natural extracts CDMO develops and manufactures drug substance derived from botanical or natural sources. Services include extraction, fractionation, purification, and GMP release testing.

What should I include in an RFQ for natural product CDMO services?

Include source material, target markers, desired potency, batch size, and target markets. Add requirements for solvent systems, residual limits, and contaminant testing.

How do natural extracts CDMOs manage raw material variability?

CDMOs implement supplier qualification, incoming testing, and standardization protocols. Ask about traceability, seasonality plans, and acceptance criteria.

What extraction methods are common for natural products?

Common methods include solvent extraction, supercritical CO2, and aqueous extraction. The method depends on the target compound and stability.

Do natural product CDMOs support purification and isolation?

Yes, many provide chromatography or crystallization to isolate active fractions. Confirm purity targets and yield expectations early.

What impurities and contaminants are critical for natural extracts?

Common concerns include pesticides, heavy metals, microbial contamination, and residual solvents. Ensure the CDMO has validated analytical methods to meet regulatory limits.

How do I evaluate GMP readiness for natural extracts?

Confirm GMP inspections, quality systems, and batch record control for natural product processing. Ask about stability studies and documentation for regulatory filings.

Can natural product CDMOs support scale-up?

Yes, many can scale extraction and purification from pilot to commercial. Verify equipment compatibility with your solvent system and target yields.

What are common pitfalls in natural extract CDMO outsourcing?

Pitfalls include insufficient raw material specifications, unclear marker compound targets, and inconsistent batch potency. Align on standardization methods early.

Do natural extracts CDMOs provide analytical characterization?

Most provide analytical testing such as HPLC, GC, and marker compound assays. Confirm which methods are validated for GMP use.

How long does natural product process development take?

Timelines depend on source material variability and extraction optimization. New botanical sources often require additional development cycles.

Do natural extracts CDMOs support regulatory documentation?

Many provide documentation for drug master files or regulatory submissions. Confirm the level of CMC support and data package formats.

How can I compare CDMO companies for natural extracts?

Compare on sourcing controls, extraction technology, purification capability, and analytical depth. Use this directory to shortlist based on region and services.

Can a CDMO handle both natural extracts and synthetic steps?

Some CDMOs combine extraction with semi-synthetic modifications. Validate whether the facility has chemical synthesis capability in addition to natural product processing.
